Definitions & Trays

التعريفات وحاملات الطبعة

📘 Definitions
TermMeaning
Impression material (مادة الطبعة)A material used for recording the form and dimensions of the oral tissues. Introduced as a plastic (soft) mass and allowed to set against the oral tissues.
Set impression (الطبعة بعد التصلب)Negative replica of the oral tissues (نسخة سالبة).
1ry impression (الطبعة الأولية)Taken for a certain patient for the first time; does not register the fine details of the oral structures.
2ry impression (الطبعة الثانوية)Taken for the same patient for the second time; records all fine details of the oral structures.
Cast (الموديل)Positive replica of the oral tissues (نسخة موجبة), obtained by pouring a suitable model material into the set impression.
Die (موديل لسن واحدة)A model for a single tooth.
Tray (حاملة الطبعة)Stock – custom (special) – tracing.

📝 الخلاصة — المعلومات الأهم

كل موضوع في كارت، وتحت كل كارت أكثر الأخطاء تكرارًا في الامتحان.

📘 Definitions

  • Set impression = negative · Cast = positive (poured into the impression) · Die = single tooth
  • 1ry = first time, no fine details · 2ry = second time, all fine details
⚠️ Cast is NOT the impression — it is poured into it.

🥄 Trays

  • Types: stock – custom (special) – tracing
  • Handle = midline · Rim lock = locks material · Perforations = retention
  • Sizes: S/M/L × Left/Right
⚠️ Don't mix the function of the rim lock with the perforations.

🔄 Sequence

  • Stock tray → 1ry impression → study cast
  • Special tray → 2ry impression → master cast & dies

✅ Requirements

  • 12 requirements (7 + 5)
  • Part 2: elastic properties · strength · dimensional stability · compatibility · disinfection
⚠️ In Lecture 2 the same list has 11 items — follow your doctor's count.

🧲 Elastic & undercuts

  • Elastic = compressed/stretched then returns to original shape
  • Undercuts: tooth (proximal) + soft tissue/bony (lingual side of ridge)
  • Removal stresses: compressive + tensile

🗂️ Classification

  • Elastic = hydrocolloids (alginate, agar) + elastomers (polysulphide, silicones, polyether)
  • Non-elastic = compound, ZOE
  • Physical = compound, wax · Chemical = ZOE, alginate, elastomers
⚠️ ZOE is non-elastic but sets CHEMICALLY; compound is non-elastic and sets PHYSICALLY.

✍️ الأسئلة المقالية المتوقعة

سؤال وإجابة مختصرة ووافية لكل سؤال محتمل في الامتحان.
✍️ س1 — Define impression material, set impression, cast and die.
  • Impression material: a material used for recording the form and dimensions of the oral tissues; introduced as a plastic (soft) mass and allowed to set against the tissues.
  • Set impression: negative replica of the oral tissues.
  • Cast: positive replica obtained by pouring a suitable model material into the set impression.
  • Die: a model for a single tooth.
✍️ س2 — Mention the ideal requirements of an impression material.
  • Pleasant odor, taste and acceptable color · Biocompatible (non-toxic, non-irritant) · Adequate shelf life · Easy to use with minimum equipment
  • Reasonable setting and working time · Adequate flow and wets oral tissues · Accepts addition and correction
  • Elastic properties · Adequate strength · Dimensional stability · Compatible with cast & die materials · Readily disinfected without loss of accuracy
✍️ س3 — Classify impression materials.
  • According to behavior after setting: Elastic (hydrocolloids: alginate, agar; elastomers: polysulphides, silicones, polyether) and Non-elastic (compound, ZOE).
  • According to setting reaction: Physical, reversible (compound, wax) and Chemical, irreversible (ZOE, alginate, elastomers).
✍️ س4 — What is meant by an elastic material? What are the types of undercuts?
  • Elastic material: can be compressed or stretched and returns to its original shape after removal of load.
  • Undercuts: tooth undercuts (proximal) and soft tissue or bony undercuts (lingual side of the ridge).
  • During removal the material is subjected to compressive and tensile stresses.
✍️ س5 — Describe the sequence from tray to die.
  • Stock tray → 1ry impression → study cast.
  • Special tray → 2ry impression.
  • Master cast and dies.

StepDetail

💡 بالعربية

  • المحفز: زيت القرنفل وصمغ وحشو ومسرّع (كلوريد الكالسيوم).
⚗️ Setting reaction — chelation
A chemical reaction called the chelation reaction.
StepReaction
1ZnO + H₂O → Zn(OH)₂
2Zn(OH)₂ + eugenol (2HE) → ZnE₂ (zinc eugenolate) + H₂O
💧 Moisture is essential: it helps the solubility of ZnO particles, changing them into zinc hydroxide, which reacts more easily with eugenol to form zinc eugenolate.
💡 بالعربية
  • تفاعل Chelation: يتحول أكسيد الزنك بالماء إلى هيدروكسيد، ثم يتفاعل مع الأوجينول فيتكوّن Zinc eugenolate.
  • الرطوبة ضرورية.

💡 بالعربية

📝 الخلاصة — المعلومات الأهم

كل موضوع في كارت، وتحت كل كارت أكثر الأخطاء تكرارًا في الامتحان.

🔥 Compound

  • Thermoplastic · non-elastic · primary impression for edentulous
  • Type I (low fusing: cake → 1ry impression, stick → tracing) · Type II (high fusing: tray)
  • 40% thermoplastic · 50% fillers · 3.3% lubricants + coloring
  • Heating 45–55°C soft · cooling 37°C hard · water bath 45°C
  • Contraction 0.3–0.5% (37 → 25°C) → pour immediately
  • Disinfect 10 min: NaOCl / chlorhexidine / iodophors
⚠️ Fillers decrease flow; they do not increase it.

⚠️ Distortion

  • Warpage = change in shape
  • Causes: incomplete softening · different temperature · delayed pouring
  • Prevention: uniform softening/thickness/cooling · avoid 16–18°C water · wash · cast early

⚗️ ZOE

  • Non-elastic · secondary impression for edentulous
  • Base: zinc oxide + mineral oils · Catalyst: oil of cloves, rosin, silica filler, calcium chloride
  • Chelation reaction: ZnO + H₂O → Zn(OH)₂, then + eugenol → zinc eugenolate + H₂O · moisture essential
  • Setting time 7–10 min · disinfect 2% glutaraldehyde
  • Acrylic special tray + green stick compound
⚠️ Pour on stone WITHOUT separating medium — the oils already act as one.

🔀 Eugenol-free ZOE

  • For eugenol allergy
  • Ethoxy benzoic acid replaces eugenol
  • Reaction = saponification · catalyst tube blue
⚠️ Eugenol-containing catalyst = red/brown; eugenol-free = blue.

🧴 ZOE handling

  • Two equal lengths on waxed pad or glass slab
  • Flexible steel spatula · sweeping rounded motion · streak free
  • Vaseline on lips and cheeks
⚠️ ZOE sticks to dry skin, lips and cheeks.

⚖️ Compound vs ZOE

  • Compound: physical, no fine details, primary, 10-min disinfection
  • ZOE: chemical, fine details, secondary, 2% glutaraldehyde
  • Both: non-elastic, no undercuts, no separating medium needed

❓ What is meant by colloids? & Tyndall effect
  • Colloids are mixtures with properties between suspensions and solutions.
  • Their particles are large enough to scatter light, but do not settle out.
  • Too small to be seen with an ordinary optical microscope; can pass through most filter papers.
  • Tyndall effect (ظاهرة تندال): colloids are detected by light scattering — sol particles absorb light energy then emit it in all directions.
💡 بالعربية

💦 Hydrocolloids
  • Colloids are called hydrocolloids when the dispersion medium is water.
  • They are used for dental impressions and exist in sol and gel states.
  • Inserted in the mouth as sol (fluid enough to record details), removed after the gel forms; they show elastic properties.
  • Sol–gel transformation: agglomeration of molecules of the dispersed phase to form connected fibrils or chains in a network pattern.

Pouring, stability & properties

الصب وثبات الأبعاد والخواص

5️⃣ Pouring & effect on gypsum
  • Pouring is done without a separating medium.
  • The water content of the impression inhibits the setting of gypsum at the surface → soft surface.
  • Hydrocolloids are hydrophilic and absorb water from the gypsum mix → chalky appearance of the models.
  • To counter: (1) immerse the impression in 2% K₂SO₄ before pouring; (2) add a gypsum accelerator in the alginate itself (potassium titanium fluoride); (3) separate the cast from the impression as soon as it sets.

📝 الخلاصة — المعلومات الأهم

كل موضوع في كارت، وتحت كل كارت أكثر الأخطاء تكرارًا في الامتحان.

🧱 Elastomers

  • Non-aqueous, elastic; 2ry impression
  • Set by polymerization (exothermic, shrinkage)
  • Electroplated (hydrophobic) except polyether
  • Solve hydrocolloid problems: tear strength, stability, electroplating
⚠️ Elastomers recover highly but NOT completely.

📏 Viscosity

  • Low/light = syringe-wash · Medium/regular + High/heavy = tray type · Putty = highest filler
  • Putty = silicones only

🔀 Polymerization types

  • Condensation: by-product, main cause of instability → polysulphide, condensation silicone
  • Addition: no by-product → addition silicone, polyether

🟤 Polysulphide

  • Base: polysulphide polymer (SH), TiO₂, dibutyl phthalate
  • Catalyst: lead dioxide (brown)
  • By-product water
  • High flexibility & tear strength, long working time, cheap
  • Messy, custom tray 2–3 mm, long setting
⚠️ Poor dimensional stability is due to the water by-product.

⚪ Condensation silicone

  • Base: poly-dimethylsiloxane (OH)
  • Catalyst: alkyl silicate + stannous octoate
  • By-product ethyl alcohol → poor stability
  • Clean, stock tray in putty-wash · hydrophobic
⚠️ Ethanol evaporation = poor stability.

🟦 Addition silicone

  • Base: polymethyl-hydrosiloxane · catalyst: divinyl polysiloxane + platinum salt
  • No by-product → high stability, multiple casts
  • H₂ gas → pinpoint voids → Pd/Pt or wait 1 h
  • Latex sulfur poisons platinum → wash or double glove
  • Hydrophilized with surfactants
⚠️ Expensive, short working time, lower tear strength.

🟪 Polyether

  • Base: aziridine-ring polyether · catalyst: aromatic sulfonic acid ester
  • Ring-opening addition, no by-product
  • Pour within one week
  • Stiff (hard to remove), bitter taste, absorbs water (no electroplating)
⚠️ Polyether is the exception that cannot be electroplated.

✍️ الأسئلة المقالية المتوقعة

سؤال وإجابة مختصرة ووافية لكل سؤال محتمل في الامتحان.
✍️ س1 — Explain polymerization and compare condensation and addition polymerization.
  • Polymerization: formation of large polymer molecules from monomers; includes chain lengthening and cross-linking; exothermic; with polymerization shrinkage.
  • Condensation: with release of a by-product (main cause of dimensional instability) — condensation silicone and polysulphide.
  • Addition: without by-product — polyether and addition silicone.
✍️ س2 — Write on polysulphide: supply, composition, setting reaction, advantages and disadvantages.
  • Two pastes in collapsible tubes. Base (white): polysulphide polymer with SH groups, TiO₂ filler, dibutyl phthalate plasticizer. Catalyst (dark brown): lead dioxide, fillers.
  • Setting: condensation polymerization, exothermic, shrinkage, water by-product.
  • Advantages: high flexibility, high tear strength, long working time, lower cost.
  • Disadvantages: poor dimensional stability, long setting time, messy (bad odor, staining), needs custom tray (2–3 mm).
✍️ س3 — Write on addition silicone: composition, setting reaction, advantages and disadvantages.
  • Base: polymethyl-hydrosiloxane. Catalyst: divinyl polysiloxane + platinum salt.
  • Setting: addition polymerization, no by-product.
  • Advantages: highly accurate, high dimensional stability, multiple casts, pleasant odor.
  • Disadvantages: expensive, short working time, hydrogen gas bubbles, hydrophobic.
✍️ س4 — What are the problems of addition silicone and how are they solved?
  • Hydrogen gas: side reaction gives pinpoint voids; solve by adding platinum or palladium scavenger or waiting 1 hour before pouring.
  • Sulfur contamination from latex gloves poisons the platinum catalyst (retardation / no polymerization, distortion); solve by washing gloves with soap and water or wearing plastic gloves over latex.
✍️ س5 — Write on polyether: composition, setting reaction, advantages and disadvantages.
  • Base: polyether with terminal aziridine rings + silica filler + plasticizer. Catalyst: aromatic sulfonic acid ester.
  • Setting: ring-opening addition polymerization, no by-product.
  • Advantages: highly accurate, good dimensional stability, good wettability, pour within one week, multiple casts.
  • Disadvantages: expensive, short working time, high stiffness, bitter taste, absorbs water.

⏱️ 1) Working & setting time
Longest → shortest: Polysulphide > Condensation silicone > Addition silicone > Polyether

💡 بالعربية
  • الأطول وقتًا Polysulphide والأقصر Polyether.
🔁 2) Rheological properties
  • All elastomers are viscoelastic with high elastic recovery if strained rapidly.
  • Best → worst elastic recovery: Addition silicone > Polyether > Condensation silicone > Polysulphide.
  • Pseudoplasticity: increasing the shearing force during mixing decreases the viscosity of polyether and addition silicone.
Clinical significance: pseudoplasticity allows manufacturers to formulate one-step (monophase) materials — when syringed they flow rapidly and adapt well to the preparation.
💡 بالعربية
  • أفضل رجوع مرن لـ Addition silicone.
  • وPseudoplasticity (قلة اللزوجة مع القص) في Polyether وAddition silicone تسمح بالمواد Monophase.
💪 3) Flexibility & stiffness
Polysulphide has the least stiffness (most flexible); polyether is the stiffest after polymerization.
Order (most → least)
StiffnessPolyether > Addition silicone > Condensation silicone > Polysulphide
FlexibilityPolysulphide > Condensation silicone > Addition silicone > Polyether
Clinical significance: stiffness matters during removal from the mouth — with loose teeth the rigidity may exfoliate them.
💡 بالعربية
  • Polysulphide الأكثر مرونة وPolyether الأكثر صلابة، وصلابته قد تخلع الأسنان المتحركة.
📐 4) Dimensional stability
  • Sources of dimensional change: (1) polymerization shrinkage; (2) loss of by-product in condensation reactions; (3) thermal contraction from oral to room temperature; (4) imbibition when exposed to water, disinfectant or high humidity → expansion.
  • Best → worst: Addition silicone > Polyether > Polysulphide > Condensation silicone.

  • Tear strength is affected by the manner of removal — rapid removal increases tear strength.
  • Most → least: Polysulphide > Addition silicone > Polyether > Condensation silicone.

  • Lack of wettability can be avoided by adding a surfactant to addition silicone.
  • Polyether has the best wettability.
  • Best → worst: Polyether > hydrophilic addition silicone > polysulphide > addition silicone = condensation silicone.
  • Shelf life: tubes and containers must be tightly closed in a cool, dry environment.

1️⃣ Multiple mix technique
  • Two mixes are made and inserted simultaneously in the mouth.
  • Equal lengths of pastes are mixed with separate spatulas on two separate paper pads: one for the syringe type (light body), one for the custom tray type (heavy body), until homogenous with no colored streaks.

  • Medium-body polyether or addition silicone is used — a single mixture.
  • Part is placed in a syringe (extrusion reduces its viscosity, pseudoplasticity) and placed around the prepared tooth.
  • The remaining material in the tray keeps its medium viscosity and forces the light body in the patient's mouth.
💡 بالعربية
  • خلطة متوسطة واحدة؛ جزء يُحقن (تقل لزوجته) والباقي في الحاملة.
3️⃣ Putty-wash technique
Used to reduce the effect of polymerization shrinkage.
  • A) Two-step (reline): putty in a stock tray makes a primary impression and acts as a custom tray for the light body → space for the wash is made by cutting away part of the putty OR placing a polyethylene sheet between the prepared teeth and putty during the primary impression → wash material is injected around the prepared tooth and over the putty.
  • B) One-step: wash material is injected around the preparation → putty in the tray is immediately seated over the wash. Disadvantage: risk of displacing too much wash material by the putty.
