Hydrocolloids (Alginate)

Applied Dental Biomaterials · 2026–2027

💡 ترجمة المصطلحات: جنب المصطلحات المهمة هتلاقي معناها بالعربي (بالشكل ده). أي مصطلح ترجمته ممكن تضيّع معناه سبناه بالإنجليزي زي ما هو.

📗 Part 1 — Colloids: background

🧪 Solution, colloid & suspension
Mixing a solute (dispersed phase) with a solvent (dispersion medium) may result in a solution, a colloid, or a suspension.
1 — Solution2 — Colloid3 — Suspension
HomogeneityHomogeneousHeterogeneousHeterogeneous
PhasesSingle phase systemTwo phase systemTwo phase system
Particle sizeSolute exists as small molecules or ions in the solvent · less than 1 nmParticle size is smaller than suspension and larger than solution · 1–1000 nmParticle size is the largest — insoluble molecules · more than 1000 nm
ExampleLike sugar in waterLike milkLike sand in water
💧 Emulsion — mixing 2 immiscible liquids
Emulsion: as a suspension, but the dispersed phase is liquid instead of solid. Example: oil in water.
❓ What is meant by colloids?
  • 1Colloids are mixtures with properties between suspensions and solutions.
  • 2Their particles are large enough to scatter light, but do not settle out.
  • 3They are too small to be seen with an ordinary optical microscope and can pass through most filter papers.
✨ Tyndall effect
Colloids can be detected by light scattering (Tyndall Effect) (ظاهرة تندال). This is because sol particles absorb light energy and then emit it in all directions in space. The phenomenon of the scattering of light by the solution particles is called the Tyndall Effect.
💦 Hydrocolloids — definition
  • 1Colloids are called hydrocolloids when the dispersion medium is water.
  • 2They are used for dental impressions.
  • 3They exist in the sol and gel state.
  • 4They are inserted in the mouth in sol state (sufficiently fluid to record details).
  • 5Then, they are removed after the gel is formed and exhibit elastic properties.
🔄 Sol-gel transformation occurs through agglomeration of molecules of dispersed phase to form connected fibrils or chains in a network pattern.
🗂️ Hydrocolloids may be:
Reversible (2ry bonds)Irreversible (1ry bonds)
AgarAlginate
Sol —(heating)→ gel · gel —(cooling)→ SolSol —(chemical reaction)→ gel
⚠️ As drawn on this slide, heating is labeled on the Sol→gel arrow and cooling on the gel→Sol arrow for Agar. Double-check this against your textbook/professor, since the usual convention is the opposite (Agar gels on cooling, liquefies on heating) — this guide follows the slide exactly as drawn.

📗 Part 2 — Alginate

📘 Definition
Alginate (الألجينات) Def: It is a viscoelastic impression material which changes from a sol to a gel state through a chemical reaction, so it is irreversible. It is indicated as a primary impression for the dentulous and edentulous patients.
📦 Forms of supply
Form
A — Bulk formIn a container with a scoop for measuring powder and plastic measuring cylinders for liquid
B — Small sealed packetsIndividually sealed pouches
🧪 Composition (7 components)
#ComponentRole
1Potassium (or sodium) alginate — 15%Soluble salt of Alginic Acid (comes from marine plants) · Main Rx ingredient because it forms sol with water
2Calcium sulfate dihydrate (CaSO₄·2H₂O) — 16%The reactor of the material — reacts with the soluble alginate form to produce an insoluble form
3Sodium phosphate Na₃(PO₄)₂Acts as a retarder — allows longer working time (Ca reacts with PO₄ before the cross-linking occurs). Its concentration determines whether it is regular or fast set material
4Potassium sulfate / Potassium titanium fluorideAccelerator for the setting time of the gypsum products used in pouring models
5FillersAs zinc oxide or diatomaceous earth — strengthens the gel
6Flavoring, coloring and disinfecting agentsE.g. chlorhexidine
7Chemical indicator (present in some products)Changes color with pH change, to indicate different stages in manipulation (chromatic alginate)
🔥 Setting reaction — gelation
The reaction of alginate is called the gelation chemical reaction.
  • 1When the powder is mixed with water, these dissociation reactions occur:
    CaSO₄ + 2H₂O → Ca + SO₄ & Na₄P₂O₇ → 4Na + P₂O₇
  • 2Then, the calcium reacts with phosphate to form insoluble calcium phosphate — (First: Retardation reaction).
  • 3This process continues until the whole amount of phosphate is depleted.
  • 4Then the calcium ions start to react with the soluble alginate (sodium or potassium alginate) to form insoluble alginate (calcium alginate) — (Second: Setting reaction): Ca + Sodium alginate → Calcium alginate (final elastic gel), where monovalent Na is replaced by divalent Ca achieving crosslinking of chains.
⚠️ Note: this slide writes the retarder's dissociation as Na₄P₂O₇ (sodium pyrophosphate), while the composition slide names it Sodium phosphate Na₃(PO₄)₂. Both are exactly as given on their respective slides — worth double-checking with your professor/textbook which formula to use in an exam.
🛠️ Manipulation — equipment
Equipment
Alginate powder
Mixing rubber bowl
Wide bladed metal spatula
Perforated or rim locked stock tray (allowing impression thickness not less than 4 mm — G.R.)
1️⃣ Dispensing and mixing
  • 1Fluff the alginate container (to evenly distribute the constituents).
  • 2W/P ratio is measured according to manufacturer instruction and placed in a clean mixing rubber bowl with a stirring motion.
  • 3Then vigorous mixing with a metal spatula against the rubber bowl wall, squeezing the material until a creamy and smooth mix is obtained.
  • 4Mixing time is 45 s to 1 min.
  • 5Avoid rocking the tray at the time of impression taking — G.R.
💡 A thin layer of alginate adhesive is applied and allowed to dry before loading the alginate (optional) — part of tray loading and impression taking.
⚖️ Manipulative factors affecting gel strength
#FactorEffect
1Too much or too little water used in mixingResults in a weak and less elastic gel
2Insufficient spatulationResults in non-uniform chemical reactions throughout the mass
3OvermixingBreaks up the calcium alginate gel network and reduces the strength
3️⃣ Impression removal
  • 1Use the alginate remaining in the bowl as an indicator for setting time.
  • 2Mixing water temperature can affect setting time — cold water slows the reaction and vice versa (safe method).
  • 3If the alginate in the bowl is set, then the material in the mouth is also set.
  • 4The impression should be removed with a snap movement — this increases the tear strength and elastic recovery.
4️⃣ Disinfection
Disinfection should be relatively rapid to prevent dimensional changes, using iodophors, sodium hypochlorite and glutaraldehyde, done by the spraying technique:
  • 1The impression is rinsed in water; the disinfectant is sprayed on the surface.
  • 2The impression is wrapped in a disinfectant-soaked paper towel and placed in a sealed plastic bag for 10 minutes.
  • 3The impression is unwrapped, rinsed, and shaken to remove excess water, then poured.
5️⃣ Pouring the impression
Pouring is done without a separating medium.
🪨 Effects on gypsum materials
Hydrocolloids affect the setting and surface properties of the gypsum materials:
  • 1The water content of the impression inhibits the setting of the gypsum at the surface and results in a soft surface.
  • 2Hydrocolloids are hydrophilic and so absorb water from the gypsum mix, which results in a chalky appearance of gypsum models.
How to counter this
  • 1Immersion of the impression in 2% K₂SO₄ before pouring (gypsum accelerator).
  • 2Incorporation of the gypsum accelerator in the alginate impression material itself, such as potassium titanium fluoride.
  • 3Separate the cast from the impression as soon as its setting occurs.
📉 Dimensional instability on storage
Causes
CauseMechanismResult
1 — Water lossBy syneresis (exuding of fluids onto the surface) or evaporationShrinkage
2 — Water uptakeBy imbibitionSwelling and expansion
How to minimize it
  • 1Pour the impression immediately (within 10 min).
  • 2If pouring must be delayed, the impression must be stored for 30–60 min maximum in various storage media such as 2% potassium SO₄.
  • 3The impression is wrapped in a surgical paper towel saturated with water, and placed in a sealed plastic bag or a humidor (100% relative humidity).

🎨 Example: Chromatic alginate is an alginate impression material with color indications avoiding confusion about setting time (e.g. BIOKALGIN) — the mix changes color through the stages of manipulation, roughly: mixed at ~5 sec, still workable at ~35 sec, firming around ~1 min 30 sec, set by about ~2 min.

📕 Lecture 1 — Introduction to Impression Materials
Impression material (مادة الطبعة)Lec 1
A material used for recording the form and dimensions of the oral tissues. Introduced as a plastic (soft) mass and allowed to set against the oral tissues.
Set impression (الطبعة بعد ما تتصلّب)Lec 1
Negative replica of the oral tissues.
1ry impression (الطبعة الأولية)Lec 1
Taken for a patient for the first time; does not register the fine details of the oral structures.
2ry impression (الطبعة الثانية)Lec 1
Taken for the same patient a second time; records all fine details of the oral structures.
Cast (الموديل)Lec 1
Positive replica of the oral tissues, obtained by pouring a suitable model material into the set impression.
Die (موديل لسنة واحدة)Lec 1
A model for a single tooth.
Tray types (حاملة الطبعة)Lec 1
Stock – custom (special) – tracing.
Elastic material (مادة مرنة)Lec 1
Capable of being compressed or stretched and returns to its original shape after removal of load (resists permanent deformation).
Classification — behavior after setting (مرنة / غير مرنة)Lec 1
Elastic = Hydrocolloids (alginate, agar) + Elastomers (polysulphides, silicones, polyether). Non-elastic = Compound, ZOE.
Classification — setting reaction (فيزيائي / كيميائي)Lec 1
Physical (reversible) = Compound, wax. Chemical (irreversible) = ZOE, alginate, elastomers.
📗 Lecture 2 — Impression Materials: Introduction, Compound & ZOE
Impression (Lecture 2 definition) (الطبعة)Lec 2
Negative replica of hard and soft oral structures.
Tray types (Lecture 2) (حاملة الطبعة)Lec 2
Stock – custom or special.
Dental compound (مادة بتلين بالحرارة وتتصلّب بالبرودة)Lec 2
A thermoplastic material that softens by heat and hardens by cooling; a non-elastic impression material used for primary impressions for edentulous patients.
Compound types (النوعين)Lec 2
Type I = low fusing · Type II = high fusing.
Compound forms (الأشكال المتاحة)Lec 2
Supplied in the form of sticks or cakes.
Compound setting reaction (تفاعل التصلب)Lec 2
Physical reversible reaction. Heating 45–55°C → soft, unset · Cooling 37°C → hard, set.
Distortion (warpage) (تغيّر في شكل الطبعة)Lec 2
Change in its shape of the compound impression. Causes: non complete homogenous softening · removal of impression from patient’s mouth to a different temperature · delaying the pouring of impression.
Zinc oxide eugenol (ZOE)Lec 2
A non-elastic impression material used for taking secondary impressions for edentulous patients.
ZOE formsLec 2
Two pastes in two collapsible tubes. Base: usually white in color. Catalyst: containing eugenol (red or brown) or eugenol-free (blue) tubes.
ZOE setting reactionLec 2
A chemical reaction called “chelation reaction”: ZnO + H₂O → Zn(OH)₂, then Zn(OH)₂ + eugenol (2HE) → ZnE₂ (zinc eugenolate) + H₂O. Presence of moisture is essential.
Eugenol-free ZOELec 2
Used for patients with eugenol allergy. Carboxylic acid derivatives are used instead of eugenol, which is “Ethoxy benzoic acid”. The reaction is called “Saponification reaction”.
📘 Lecture 3 — Hydrocolloids (Alginate)
Hydrocolloid (الغرويات المائية)Lec 3
A colloid whose dispersion medium is water. Exists in sol and gel state; inserted in the mouth as sol, removed as gel.
Agar vs alginate (المرن والغير مرن للجيلاتين)Lec 3
Agar = reversible (2ry bonds), sol ⇌ gel by heating/cooling. Alginate = irreversible (1ry bonds), sol → gel by chemical reaction.
Alginate (تعريف الألجينات)Lec 3
A viscoelastic impression material; sol → gel via a chemical reaction (irreversible); primary impression for dentulous and edentulous patients.
Tyndall effect (ظاهرة تندال)Lec 3
Colloids are detected by light scattering: sol particles absorb light energy and emit it in all directions.
Alginate setting reaction (تفاعل الـ Gelation)Lec 3
Called the gelation chemical reaction. 1st: Ca + phosphate → insoluble calcium phosphate (retardation). 2nd: Ca + sodium alginate → calcium alginate, the final elastic gel (setting reaction).
Dimensional instability (alginate) (عدم الثبات الأبعادي)Lec 3
Water loss (syneresis/evaporation) → shrinkage. Water uptake (imbibition) → swelling and expansion.

🧠 Self-Test — اختبر نفسك
جاوب في دماغك الأول، بعدين دوس تتأكد. (Active Recall)
📕 Lecture 1
❓ What is a set impression?
✅ Negative replica of the oral tissues.
❓ What is a cast, and how is it obtained?
✅ Positive replica of the oral tissues, obtained by pouring a suitable model material into the set impression.
❓ What is a die?
✅ A model for a single tooth.
❓ What is the difference between a 1ry and a 2ry impression?
✅ 1ry: taken for the first time, does not register the fine details. 2ry: taken for the same patient a second time, records all fine details.
❓ Name the three types of tray (Lecture 1).
✅ Stock – custom (special) – tracing.
❓ Which materials are classified as elastic, according to behavior after setting?
✅ Hydrocolloids (alginate, agar) and elastomers (polysulphides, silicones, polyether).
❓ Which materials set by a chemical (irreversible) reaction?
✅ ZOE, alginate and elastomers.
❓ Which materials set by a physical (reversible) reaction?
✅ Compound and wax.
📗 Lecture 2
❓ What is dental compound, and which impressions is it used for?
✅ A thermoplastic material that softens by heat and hardens by cooling; a non-elastic material used for primary impressions for edentulous patients.
❓ What temperature softens compound, and what temperature hardens it?
✅ Heating (45–55°C) → soft, unset. Cooling (37°C) → hard, set.
❓ What percentage of compound is fillers, and what do they do?
✅ 50% — they decrease the flow, increase strength and give body to the mixture (like talc).
❓ What is distortion (warpage), and what are its three causes?
✅ Change in its shape. Causes: non complete homogenous softening, removal of impression from patient’s mouth to a different temperature, and delaying the pouring of impression.
❓ What is ZOE, and how is it supplied?
✅ A non-elastic impression material for secondary impressions for edentulous patients; two pastes in two collapsible tubes (base usually white; catalyst red or brown with eugenol, or blue if eugenol-free).
❓ What is the setting reaction of ZOE, and why is moisture essential?
✅ A chemical reaction called chelation reaction. Moisture helps the solubility of zinc oxide particles, changing it into zinc hydroxide, which is easier to react with eugenol to form zinc eugenolate.
❓ When is eugenol-free ZOE used, and what replaces eugenol?
✅ For patients with eugenol allergy. Carboxylic acid derivatives (ethoxy benzoic acid) are used instead of eugenol, and the reaction is called saponification reaction.
❓ Which tray is used for ZOE, what is the setting time, and how is it disinfected?
✅ Acrylic special tray traced with green stick compound (the impression has high flow); setting time about 7–10 minutes; disinfection by immersion in 2% glutaraldehyde.
📘 Lecture 3
❓ What is a colloid, in terms of particle size, compared to a solution and a suspension?
✅ A colloid has particles smaller than a suspension and larger than a solution (roughly 1-1000 nm).
❓ What is the Tyndall effect?
✅ The scattering of light by sol particles, which absorb light energy and emit it in all directions — this is how colloids are detected.
❓ What is a hydrocolloid, and in which two states does it exist?
✅ A colloid whose dispersion medium is water; it exists in the sol and gel state, inserted into the mouth as sol and removed as gel.
❓ What is the difference between Agar and Alginate as hydrocolloids?
✅ Agar is reversible (2ry bonds), changing between sol and gel by heating and cooling. Alginate is irreversible (1ry bonds), changing from sol to gel by a chemical reaction.
❓ What is alginate, and which impression is it indicated for?
✅ A viscoelastic impression material that changes from sol to gel through a chemical reaction (irreversible); indicated as a primary impression for dentulous and edentulous patients.
❓ What are the two main reactive components of alginate powder, and their roles?
✅ Calcium sulfate dihydrate (16%), the reactor, and sodium phosphate, the retarder (allows longer working time).
❓ What are the two steps of the alginate setting (gelation) reaction?
✅ First, the retardation reaction: calcium reacts with phosphate to form insoluble calcium phosphate, until the phosphate is depleted. Second, the setting reaction: calcium reacts with sodium alginate to form calcium alginate, the final elastic gel.
❓ What is the minimum impression thickness the stock tray should allow for alginate, and what should be avoided during mixing?
✅ Not less than 4 mm. Rocking the tray at the time of impression taking should be avoided.
❓ How should an alginate impression be removed from the mouth, and why?
✅ With a snap movement, because this increases the tear strength and elastic recovery.
❓ How is an alginate impression disinfected by the spraying technique?
✅ Rinse in water, spray disinfectant on the surface, wrap in a disinfectant-soaked paper towel, place in a sealed plastic bag for 10 minutes, then unwrap, rinse and shake before pouring.
❓ Why do alginate impressions cause a chalky appearance on gypsum models, and how can this be countered?
✅ Alginate is hydrophilic and absorbs water from the gypsum mix. It can be countered by immersing the impression in 2% K2SO4 before pouring, adding a gypsum accelerator to the alginate itself, or separating the cast as soon as it sets.
❓ What causes dimensional instability of alginate on storage, and how can it be minimized?
✅ Water loss (syneresis or evaporation) causes shrinkage; water uptake (imbibition) causes swelling. It is minimized by pouring within 10 minutes, or if delayed, storing for 30-60 minutes maximum in a humid storage medium.
❓ What is the elastic recovery of alginate, and what is its tear strength?
✅ Elastic recovery is 97.3%; tear strength is poor.
❓ Name the 5 modified forms of alginate.
✅ Two pastes system, dust free alginate, siliconized alginate, alginate containing disinfectants, and chromatic alginate.
